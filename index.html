A cached answer you never audited is a liability with a discount

Serving-cost pages treat cache pricing as pure win: the same question twice bills the second read at a tenth of the first. What the pricing page leaves out is who owns the answer once it sits in the cache. The audit shape at PastAGI, where every cost claim gets replayed against real traffic, applies to the cache twice over, because a cached response carries both a price and an age.

Take a policy-bot billed at $3 per million input tokens with prompt caching on. The cold path is a 5,000-token call, system prompt and question together, billed fresh. Warm, the platform reuses 3,900 tokens of the prefix at a 90 percent discount and reads only the changed 1,100 at full price, so a warm call costs 0.45 cents against 1.5 cents cold. One thousand cold calls a day run $15.00. The same thousand calls, 70 percent warm, run $7.63. The saving is $7.37 per day, and the cached share of those answers has quietly stopped being re-read by anyone.

Now change the policy document. Every pricing guide prints the 90 percent figure next to the discount and stops there. The audit question is the warm fraction itself: with a 24-hour cache window, what share of production calls hit a prefix that predates the current policy? Nobody on the team can answer it, because the dashboard shows hit rate and nothing else. Two revisions land in a busy month and the warm fraction is carrying answers composed against the old document for hours until someone notices the bot citing a section that no longer exists.

The check costs one query: log prefix age at call time and read the distribution weekly. If the 95th-percentile prefix is older than the last content revision, the discount came out of correctness, and the fix is a cache version bump wired to the publish step. That same week is also when the stale-answer complaints stop.

Price the cache the way the rest of the ledger is priced: dollars saved per warm call against dollars at risk per stale one. The discount is real, and it is a tenth of the story.
